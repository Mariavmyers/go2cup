Where to Watch the Liga MX Liguilla in the USA (Apertura 2026)

Channels, apps, dates and the tiebreak rules for the Apertura 2026 quarterfinals, semifinals and final, for fans in the US.

SportDGX Insider · November 24, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer: there's no single channel for the Liguilla in the US. It depends on the home team. Most games air on TUDN, Univision, UniMás and ViX. Chivas home games air on Telemundo and Peacock. Monterrey and Santos home games air on FOX channels. For English commentary, some games air on CBS Sports Network and Paramount+.

Liguilla key dates

  • No Play-In: the regular season ended Nov. 22 (Matchday 17) and the top 8 went straight to the quarterfinals.
  • Quarterfinals: first legs Nov. 25–26, second legs Nov. 28–29.
  • Semifinals: first legs Dec. 2–3, second legs that weekend.
  • Final: Dec. 10 and 13. Heads up: if Toluca makes the final, it moves to Dec. 24 and 27 because of the Intercontinental Cup.

Dates and kickoff times can change. Check the official schedule on game day.

How the Liguilla works (in plain English)

The Liguilla is Liga MX's playoff, played at the end of each of its two tournaments a year: the Apertura (fall) and the Clausura (spring). Each club gets its own champion, so the Apertura 2026 title is decided in December.

This season, eight teams made it. There was no Play-In round this time: the top eight clubs in the standings after 17 matchdays went straight into the quarterfinals. The top seed plays the eighth seed, second plays seventh, third plays sixth, and fourth plays fifth.

Two legs, one aggregate score

Every round is played over two games, called "legs": one at each team's stadium. What matters is the aggregate score, meaning the goals from both games added together. If a team wins the first leg 2-1 and loses the second 1-0, the aggregate is 2-2.

What happens if the aggregate is tied?

This is the rule that surprises most new fans. In the quarterfinals and semifinals, there is no extra time and no penalty shootout. If the aggregate is tied after 180 minutes, the team that finished higher in the regular-season standings goes through. There is also no away-goals rule.

That is why the higher seed often plays the second leg at home and can sometimes play for a draw: a tie is enough for them.

The final is different. If the aggregate is tied after both legs, the teams play extra time (two 15-minute halves) and, if it is still tied, a penalty shootout.

Quick rule: quarterfinals and semifinals: a tie goes to the better seed. Final: a tie goes to extra time, then penalties.

Why the final could land on Christmas Eve

The final is scheduled for Thursday, Dec. 10 and Sunday, Dec. 13. But Toluca, the defending champion, is also playing in FIFA's Intercontinental Cup, which starts Dec. 9. If Toluca reaches the Liga MX final, the league moves the final to Thursday, Dec. 24 and Sunday, Dec. 27.

So if you are planning a watch party, a trip to Mexico or a family gathering around the final, wait until the semifinals finish before you lock in dates.

How to watch without cable

  • Free with an antenna: Univision, UniMás, Telemundo and FOX are over-the-air channels, so some games are free with a TV antenna.
  • ViX Premium: TelevisaUnivision's app, with most games.
  • Peacock: for Chivas home games.
  • Live TV streaming: DirecTV Stream includes Univision, TUDN and FOX. Fubo no longer carries Univision, UniMás or TUDN, so it misses many games.
  • Check current prices at sign-up; many services offer a free trial.

How to pick the right way to watch

Because US rights are split by home team, the best option depends on which club you follow. A simple way to decide:

Remember: in a two-leg series each club hosts once, so the first and second legs of the same matchup can be on different channels.

Game-day checklist

  • Check the home team first. The home club decides the channel for that leg.
  • Confirm the kickoff time the day of the game. Liga MX publishes the official schedule for each round only after the previous round ends, and times can move.
  • Sign in to your app early. Streaming apps get busy right before big games; log in and update the app before kickoff.
  • Using an antenna? Run a channel scan a day before so you know Univision, UniMás, Telemundo and FOX come in clearly where you live.
  • Free trials: if you use one to watch a series, set a reminder to cancel before it renews, unless you want to keep it.

Watching with other fans

Liguilla nights are some of the best soccer atmospheres in the US, and many Mexican restaurants and sports bars in cities like Kansas City, Dallas, Houston, Chicago and Los Angeles show the big games. Call ahead and ask which match they will have on, especially when two series are played the same night.

What comes next

After the quarterfinals (Nov. 25–29), the four winners move on and the semifinals are played Dec. 2–3 with second legs that weekend. The final follows on Dec. 10 and 13, or Dec. 24 and 27 if Toluca is in it. SportDGX will publish where-to-watch guides for each round.

FAQ

Is there a Play-In this season?

No. The top 8 teams after 17 matchdays went straight to the quarterfinals.

What happens if a Liguilla series is tied on aggregate?

In the quarterfinals and semifinals, the team that finished higher in the regular-season table advances. There is no extra time, no penalties and no away-goals rule. In the final, a tie goes to extra time and then penalties.

Can I watch the Liguilla in English?

Some games, on CBS Sports Network and Paramount+.

Does Fubo have TUDN?

No. Fubo no longer carries Univision, UniMás or TUDN.

When is the Apertura 2026 final?

Dec. 10 and 13; if Toluca reaches the final, it moves to Dec. 24 and 27.
