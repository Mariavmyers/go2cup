Women's World Cup 2027 Tickets: How to Buy From FIFA

Sales open December 1: the dates, the three ticket packages, starting prices and how to buy safely from FIFA's official site.

SportDGX Insider · November 27, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: tickets for the FIFA Women's World Cup 2027 in Brazil go on sale on Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. Brazil time (8:00 a.m. Eastern, 7:00 a.m. Central). The first phase runs until December 8, sells packages only on a first-come, first-served basis, and is available only at FIFA.com/tickets. FIFA says package prices start from about BRL 30 (around USD 5) per match.

The ticket timeline at a glance

FIFA announced the plan in a media release on November 13, 2026. The sale is split into four phases. Only the first one has fixed dates so far, and FIFA has said the rest will follow the final draw on Friday, December 11, 2026, in Rio de Janeiro.

PhaseWhenWhat is on sale
1. Opening saleDec 1 – Dec 8, 2026Three package types, first come, first served
Final drawDec 11, 2026Groups set; schedule and kickoff times confirmed after
2. Visa presaleAfter the draw (date not yet announced)Open only to Visa cardholders
3 and 4. Later salesNot yet announcedTwo more phases, details to come

Some websites list a firm date for the Visa presale. FIFA's own release only says it opens after the draw and the confirmation of the match schedule, so treat any specific date as unconfirmed until FIFA publishes it.

How the first sales phase works

The opening phase is different from what many fans remember from recent men's tournaments. There is no random draw (lottery) announced for this phase. Instead, packages sell in the order people buy them until FIFA's first-phase allocation runs out. That means the start time matters: if you want a specific package, be ready at 10:00 a.m. Brazil time on December 1.

What "Brazil time" means for US fans

Brasília time is three hours behind UTC, and Brazil does not currently use daylight saving time. On December 1 that works out to:

  • Eastern: 8:00 a.m.
  • Central: 7:00 a.m.
  • Mountain: 6:00 a.m.
  • Pacific: 5:00 a.m.

FIFA's release describes the start as 10:00 local time, which it lists as 14:00 Central European Time. All of the conversions above line up with that.

Which seats are available

FIFA says categories 1, 2 and 3 are offered in the opening phase. Category 1 is usually the best view along the sidelines, and category 3 is usually higher up or behind the goals. The lowest prices FIFA quoted are for category 3. All prices include tax and are charged in Brazilian reais (BRL), so your bank may add a foreign transaction fee and the dollar amount will move with the exchange rate.

The three ticket packages explained

In phase 1 you cannot buy a single ticket for one match. You buy one of three packages instead. Here is what FIFA says each one includes.

PackageWhat you getStarting price (FIFA)
Brazil Team PassAll three of Brazil's group-stage matchesFrom BRL 205 (about USD 36), category 3
Arena PassMatches at one stadium, up to and including the quarterfinals where that venue hosts themFIFA says about 27% cheaper than buying the same tickets one by one
Flex PassAny three eligible matches; can include one Brazil group match or one semifinalThree matches can cost under BRL 100 (about USD 18)

Brazil Team Pass

This is the package for fans who want to follow the host nation. Brazil plays its group games on June 24 at the Maracanã in Rio, on June 29 in São Paulo and on July 4 in Brasília, so this pass also means traveling between three cities in about ten days. Expect strong demand from Brazilian fans.

Arena Pass

The Arena Pass suits fans who want to base themselves in one city. Pick the stadium, and you get its matches through the furthest round it hosts before the semifinals. Four cities (Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, Belo Horizonte and Fortaleza) host quarterfinals, while Brasília, Salvador, Recife and Porto Alegre stop at the round of 16. Our host cities and stadiums guide breaks down which rounds each venue gets.

Flex Pass

The Flex Pass is the most flexible choice: three matches of your choosing from the eligible list. It can include one Brazil group game or one semifinal, but not the final or the third-place match. It is a good fit if you plan to visit two or three cities on one trip.

Before the draw, nobody knows exactly which teams will play in which stadium. If you buy an Arena Pass or Flex Pass in December, you are choosing cities and dates, not opponents, unless the match involves Brazil.

Step by step: how to buy

  1. Create a FIFA ID now. FIFA invites fans to make an account in advance and register interest at FIFA.com/tickets. Doing it before December 1 saves time on sale day.
  2. Choose your package and a backup. Decide which package and seat category you want, and pick a second option in case your first choice sells out.
  3. Check your card. Visa is FIFA's official payment partner and its preferred method. In phase 1, FIFA says Visa credit and debit cards are accepted along with other payment methods. Tell your bank you will make a purchase in Brazilian reais so it does not block the charge.
  4. Be online before 10:00 a.m. Brazil time on December 1. Use a steady internet connection and a single browser window logged in to your FIFA ID.
  5. Complete checkout and save your confirmation. Keep the confirmation email and check your FIFA account for updates on how and when tickets will be delivered.
  6. Watch the December 11 draw. If you miss out in phase 1, the next chances come after the draw.

Half-price tickets, accessibility and hospitality

Half-price (meia-entrada) tickets

Brazilian law gives certain groups the right to half-price tickets for cultural and sporting events. It is known locally as meia-entrada. FIFA's release confirms half-price tickets will be offered to eligible groups in line with Brazilian law, but it did not list who qualifies or which documents are needed. Under Brazil's general rules, those groups typically include students, people aged 60 and over and people with disabilities, but the proof required is usually Brazilian documentation, so most visitors from the US should plan on paying the full price. Check FIFA's ticketing terms before you buy.

Accessible seating

FIFA says accessible tickets are available from the very start of sales, including spaces for wheelchair users and extra-width seats. Eligible fans can also request a companion ticket.

Hospitality packages

Premium hospitality (seats with food, drinks and lounge access) is sold separately and is not part of the December 1 sale. FIFA says hospitality timelines, packages and prices will be released soon, and fans can register interest at FIFA.com/hospitality.

How to avoid ticket scams

FIFA sells Women's World Cup 2027 tickets exclusively online at FIFA.com/tickets and specifically warns fans to stay away from unauthorized websites, social media sellers and other middlemen. A few simple rules keep you safe:

  • Type the address yourself. Go to FIFA.com/tickets directly instead of clicking links in ads, texts or social media posts.
  • Ignore "guaranteed" tickets before the sale opens. Nobody outside FIFA can sell you a real ticket for a package that is not on sale yet.
  • Be wary of sellers who ask for wire transfers, gift cards or payment apps. Those payments are almost impossible to get back.
  • Expect official updates from FIFA. FIFA has said more sales phases are coming, so a sold-out package in December does not mean the tournament is sold out.

Key takeaways

  • Sale opens: Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. Brazil time (8:00 a.m. ET).
  • Phase 1 ends: December 8, or earlier for any package that sells out.
  • What you can buy: Brazil Team Pass, Arena Pass or Flex Pass. No single-match tickets in phase 1.
  • Lowest prices: from about BRL 30 (USD 5) per match in category 3, according to FIFA.
  • Where: FIFA.com/tickets only. Payments are in Brazilian reais.
  • Next big date: the final draw on December 11, followed by a Visa cardholder presale.

What to do next

If you are seriously thinking about going to Brazil next summer, do three things this weekend: create your FIFA ID, decide which package fits your trip, and set an alarm for December 1. Then check Brazil's e-visa rules, since US citizens need a visa to visit, and start looking at flights into Rio de Janeiro or São Paulo. The tournament runs from June 24 to July 25, 2027. FAQ

When do Women's World Cup 2027 tickets go on sale?

Tickets go on sale on Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. Brazil time, which is 8:00 a.m. Eastern in the US. The first phase runs until December 8.

Where can I buy Women's World Cup 2027 tickets?

FIFA sells Women's World Cup 2027 tickets only online at FIFA.com/tickets. FIFA warns fans to avoid unauthorized websites, social media sellers and other intermediaries.

How much do Women's World Cup 2027 tickets cost?

FIFA says packages start from about BRL 30 (around USD 5) per match in category 3. The Brazil Team Pass starts from BRL 205 (about USD 36), and all prices are charged in Brazilian reais with tax included.

Can I buy a single match ticket in the first sales phase?

No. The first phase, from December 1 to 8, 2026, sells only three packages: the Brazil Team Pass, the Arena Pass and the Flex Pass. FIFA says more sales phases will follow the December 11 draw.

Is there a lottery for Women's World Cup 2027 tickets?

FIFA has not announced a lottery for the first phase. Packages are sold on a first-come, first-served basis until the first-phase allocation runs out.

Do I need a Visa card to buy tickets?

Not in the first phase, where FIFA accepts Visa credit and debit cards alongside other payment methods. The second phase, after the draw, is a presale open only to Visa cardholders.
