NFL Playoff Tiebreakers Explained: How Ties Are Broken

How the NFL breaks ties for division titles, wild cards and seeds, in plain English, with the Chiefs-Broncos race in the AFC West as a real example.

SportDGX Insider · November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: when two NFL teams finish with the same record, the league breaks the tie with a fixed list of steps. For teams in the same division, it goes head-to-head first, then record in division games, then common games, then conference games, then strength of victory and strength of schedule. For wild-card spots between teams from different divisions, conference record moves up to second place. A coin toss is the very last step, and it almost never comes up.

Why tiebreakers matter so much this year

With 17 games and only 14 playoff spots, ties in the standings are common. They decide who wins a division, who grabs the last wild card, and who earns the single first-round bye in each conference.

The AFC West is a perfect example right now. After games through Nov. 22, the Kansas City Chiefs and Denver Broncos were both 8-2. The Chiefs beat Denver 31-10 at Arrowhead on Sept. 14. Denver won the rematch 28-24 in Denver on Nov. 1, scoring 15 points in the fourth quarter. So the season series is split 1-1, and head-to-head no longer separates them. That is why you may see standings sites list the two teams in a different order from week to week.

Kansas City's next test comes tonight, Thanksgiving night, at Buffalo (NBC and Peacock). Denver plays at Pittsburgh on Friday, Nov. 27 (Prime Video). Every result shifts the tiebreaker math below. For the full Chiefs schedule and all 32 teams, see our NFL hub.

How NFL playoff seeding works

Each conference (AFC and NFC) sends seven teams to the playoffs:

  • Seeds 1–4: the four division winners, ranked by record. A division winner gets one of these seeds even if a wild-card team has a better record.
  • Seeds 5–7: the three wild cards, the best remaining records in the conference, ranked by record.
  • The No. 1 seed only gets a bye through the first round (called Wild Card Weekend). Seeds 2 through 7 all play that weekend.

Higher seeds host every playoff game until the Super Bowl, which is at a neutral site. That is why seeding ties matter even for teams that have already clinched a spot: the difference between the 1 seed and the 2 seed is a week of rest and home field all the way through.

Was the seeding system changed for 2026?

No. In 2025, a proposal to rank playoff teams purely by record (so division winners would no longer be guaranteed a home game) was tabled and then withdrawn. Reporting in February 2026 said the idea would not come back until the regular season expands. For the 2026 playoffs, division winners still get the top four seeds.

Two teams in the same division

This is the one that matters for Chiefs vs. Broncos. The NFL goes down this list in order and stops at the first step that separates the teams:

  1. Head-to-head record between the two teams.
  2. Record in division games (the six games against division rivals).
  3. Record in common games, meaning games against opponents both teams played.
  4. Record in conference games.
  5. Strength of victory.
  6. Strength of schedule.
  7. Combined ranking among conference teams in points scored and points allowed.
  8. Combined ranking among all teams in points scored and points allowed.
  9. Net points in common games (points scored minus points allowed).
  10. Net points in all games.
  11. Net touchdowns in all games.
  12. Coin toss.

Because Kansas City and Denver split their two games, step 1 is a tie. The race now goes to step 2, division record. Division records are not final until Week 18, so this is not settled. If they are still level after division games, the next stop is common opponents, and division rivals share most of their schedule.

Two teams fighting for a wild card

If two tied teams come from the same division, the league simply uses the division list above. If they come from different divisions, it uses a separate wild-card list:

StepSame divisionWild card (different divisions)
1Head-to-headHead-to-head, if they played
2Division recordConference record
3Common gamesCommon games (minimum of four)
4Conference recordStrength of victory
5Strength of victoryStrength of schedule
6Strength of scheduleConference points ranking
LastCoin tossCoin toss

Two details trip people up. First, teams from different divisions often never play each other, so step 1 is frequently skipped. Second, the common-games step only counts if the teams have at least four common opponents. If they have fewer, the league moves on to the next step.

A simple made-up example

Here is a hypothetical, not a prediction. Say Team A from the AFC East and Team B from the AFC South both finish 11-6 and are fighting for the last wild card. They did not play each other, so step 1 is skipped. Team A went 8-4 against AFC teams and Team B went 7-5. Team A gets the spot on step 2, conference record, and the league never needs to look at common games or strength of victory. Most real ties end this early, within the first three steps.

The same wild-card list is also used to rank division winners against each other for seeds 1–4. So if the Chiefs or Broncos win the West and tie, say, Jacksonville for the No. 1 seed, conference record becomes very important.

Three or more teams tied

Three-way ties sound scary but follow simple logic.

Inside one division

The league uses the same 12 steps, but step 1 becomes the combined head-to-head record among all the tied teams. As soon as one team is eliminated and two remain, the process starts over at step 1 of the two-team list. This "revert to step 1" rule surprises a lot of fans.

For wild cards

  1. First, if two or more of the tied teams are from the same division, the league uses the division tiebreaker to keep only the highest-ranked team from each division.
  2. Head-to-head counts only if it is a sweep: one team beat every other tied team, or lost to every one of them.
  3. Then conference record, common games (minimum four), strength of victory, strength of schedule, the points rankings, net points, net touchdowns and the coin toss.

Only one team advances at each step. Once a wild card is filled, the league starts the process again from the top to fill the next one.

The jargon, explained simply

  • Strength of victory: the combined winning percentage of the teams you beat. Beating good teams helps more than beating bad ones.
  • Strength of schedule: the combined winning percentage of all your opponents, wins and losses.
  • Common games: your record only against opponents both tied teams played. The league uses winning percentage, because the teams may have played a different number of games against those opponents.
  • Points ranking: the league ranks teams in points scored and points allowed, then adds the two ranks. The lower total wins.
  • Tie games: a tied game counts as half a win and half a loss for both teams.
  • Clinch / eliminated: a team has "clinched" when no combination of results can knock it out, and is "eliminated" when no combination can get it in.

Quick facts

  • 14 teams make the NFL playoffs, seven from each conference.
  • Division winners take seeds 1–4; wild cards take seeds 5–7.
  • Only the No. 1 seed in each conference gets a first-round bye.
  • Division ties: head-to-head, then division record.
  • Wild-card ties between divisions: head-to-head (if played), then conference record.
  • Chiefs and Broncos split their 2026 games: KC won 31-10 on Sept. 14, Denver won 28-24 on Nov. 1.
  • The coin toss exists in the rules but is a true last resort.

What to watch next

For Chiefs fans, the questions are simple: how do Kansas City and Denver finish in division games, and how do they do against the rest of the AFC? Those two records are likely to decide the AFC West and possibly the No. 1 seed. Kansas City's December stretch includes the Rams on Dec. 3, the Bengals on Dec. 13 and a trip to New England on Dec. 21.

If teams are from different divisions, look at conference record. And remember that the standings you see in late November are a snapshot; most ties sort themselves out by Week 18.

FAQ

What is the first NFL tiebreaker?

Head-to-head record is the first tiebreaker for two teams in the same division. For a wild card between teams from different divisions, head-to-head is used only if the teams played each other.

What happens if teams split their season series?

If two division rivals split their two games, the next tiebreaker is record in division games. If that is also tied, the league looks at record against common opponents, then conference record.

What is strength of victory in the NFL?

Strength of victory is the combined winning percentage of the teams a club has beaten. It rewards teams whose wins came against better opponents.

How many teams make the NFL playoffs?

Fourteen teams make the NFL playoffs, seven from each conference. The four division winners get seeds 1 to 4 and three wild cards get seeds 5 to 7, and only the No. 1 seed gets a first-round bye.

Does the NFL ever use a coin toss to break a tie?

A coin toss is the final step in every NFL tiebreaker list. It is only used if every other step, including net points and net touchdowns, still leaves the teams tied, which is extremely rare.

Did the Chiefs and Broncos split their 2026 games?

Yes. Kansas City beat Denver 31-10 at Arrowhead on Sept. 14, 2026, and Denver won 28-24 in Denver on Nov. 1, 2026, so head-to-head does not break a tie between them.
