Women's World Cup 2027 Host Cities and Stadiums Guide

All eight Brazilian host cities and stadiums for the 2027 Women's World Cup, which rounds each one hosts, and how to plan a trip around them.

SportDGX Insider · November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: the FIFA Women's World Cup 2027 runs June 24 to July 25, 2027 in eight Brazilian host cities: Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, Belo Horizonte, Brasília, Fortaleza, Salvador, Recife and Porto Alegre. Rio's Maracanã hosts both the opening match and the final. All eight stadiums were also used at the men's 2014 World Cup.

The eight host cities at a glance

FIFA picked the host cities in May 2025 from twelve Brazilian candidates. Cuiabá, Manaus, Natal and Belém were left out. In August 2026, FIFA published the match schedule: 64 matches spread across the eight cities, with every city getting at least seven games and at least one knockout match.

Here is how the venues line up. Capacities are approximate and come from public stadium listings; FIFA may set different numbers for the tournament.

Rio de Janeiro: Maracanã, the opener and the final

The Maracanã is the headline venue. Brazil, the host nation, plays the opening match there on Thursday, June 24, 2027, and the stadium also hosts one semifinal and the final on July 25.

That makes the Maracanã only the third stadium in the world to host both a men's World Cup final and a Women's World Cup final. The other two are Råsunda in Sweden and the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, California, which hosted the 1994 men's final and the 1999 women's final won by the USA.

Getting there

The stadium sits in the North Zone of Rio, away from the beach neighborhoods most visitors stay in. The city's metro and SuperVia train both have a Maracanã station right next to the ground, which is usually the easiest way in and out. Many fans stay in Copacabana, Ipanema, Botafogo or the city center and ride the metro to the game.

São Paulo: Arena Itaquera and the knockout rounds

São Paulo is Brazil's biggest city and its main air hub, with Guarulhos (GRU) the most common arrival airport for flights from the US. Its venue, Arena Itaquera, is the home of Corinthians and opened the 2014 men's World Cup.

Brazil plays its second group game here on Tuesday, June 29. Later, the stadium hosts one of the two semifinals and the third-place match, so São Paulo will be busy deep into the tournament.

Getting there

The stadium is in the far east of the city, next to the Corinthians-Itaquera station on Metro Line 3 (Red) and the CPTM commuter rail. São Paulo traffic is famously slow, so plan on the train rather than a car on match day.

Belo Horizonte and Fortaleza: quarterfinal cities

Beyond Rio and São Paulo, only two cities get quarterfinals: Belo Horizonte and Fortaleza.

Belo Horizonte (Mineirão)

The capital of Minas Gerais state is known for its food scene and its bar culture. The Mineirão is a big, classic bowl in the Pampulha area, home to Cruzeiro and Atlético Mineiro. It sits some distance from downtown, so most fans use buses, rideshare or organized shuttles on match days.

Fortaleza (Arena Castelão)

Fortaleza is a beach city on Brazil's northeast coast, close to the equator. The Castelão hosted six games at the 2014 men's World Cup. Expect warm, sunny weather and a long flight from the south of the country.

Brasília, Salvador, Recife and Porto Alegre: group stage and round of 16

These four cities host group games and matches up to the round of 16, the first knockout round in a 32-team tournament. For fans on a budget, these early-round games are often the best chance to see top players live.

  • Brasília: the planned capital city, famous for its modernist architecture. Brazil plays its third group game here on Sunday, July 4. The national stadium is the largest venue after the Maracanã.
  • Salvador: the historic capital of Bahia, with the Pelourinho old town and a strong Afro-Brazilian culture. Arena Fonte Nova is close to the center.
  • Recife: a coastal city in the northeast. Arena Pernambuco is actually in the neighboring town of São Lourenço da Mata, west of Recife, so allow extra travel time.
  • Porto Alegre: the southernmost host city, near the borders with Uruguay and Argentina. Beira-Rio, home of Internacional, sits on the Guaíba waterfront.

Planning a trip around the host cities

Brazil is about the size of the continental United States, and that shapes every travel plan for this tournament.

Distances are big

Rio and São Paulo are only about 400 km (250 miles) apart, and there are frequent short flights and buses between them. But Porto Alegre in the south and Fortaleza in the north are more than 3,000 km (about 2,000 miles) apart. Following one team across the group stage will usually mean domestic flights, so book them early once the schedule for your team is clear.

June and July are winter in Brazil

The tournament is played in the Southern Hemisphere winter, but "winter" means very different things across the country.

  • Porto Alegre can be chilly, with cool or cold nights. Pack a jacket.
  • São Paulo, Belo Horizonte and Brasília are usually mild, and Brasília is typically dry at this time of year.
  • Rio is generally pleasant, with warm days and cooler evenings.
  • Fortaleza, Salvador and Recife stay warm. The east coast of the northeast can get heavy rain in this season, so pack a light rain layer.

Entry rules for US travelers

Brazil now requires a visa for US citizens, available through an online e-visa process. Requirements and fees can change, so check the official Brazilian government or consulate website, and the US State Department's Brazil page, well before you book.

Key dates to know

  1. December 11, 2026: the final draw at the Museum of Modern Art in Rio de Janeiro. This decides the groups and tells fans which cities their team plays in.
  2. June 24, 2027: opening match, Brazil at the Maracanã.
  3. June 29 and July 4, 2027: Brazil's other group games in São Paulo and Brasília.
  4. Round of 16, quarterfinals and semifinals: in July, between the end of the group stage and the final. Exact dates and matchups follow the draw.
  5. July 25, 2027: the final at the Maracanã.

FIFA will publish an updated schedule with team names after the draw. Kickoff times have not all been confirmed yet.

Tickets: use official FIFA channels only

FIFA has opened a way for fans to register their interest in tickets at FIFA.com/tickets. General sale dates and prices had not been announced when this article was published. When sales open, buy only through FIFA's official platform. Unofficial resale sites can charge large markups and may sell tickets that do not work at the gate. Reports in Brazil also say some local students, older adults and low-income residents are expected to get half-price tickets under Brazilian law.

Quick facts

  • Edition: the 10th FIFA Women's World Cup and the first in South America.
  • Teams: 32, in eight groups of four. The top two in each group reach the round of 16.
  • Matches: 64 across eight cities.
  • Defending champion: Spain, winner of the 2023 tournament in Australia and New Zealand.
  • Most titles: the USA, with four.
  • Host vote: Brazil beat a joint Belgium, Germany and Netherlands bid at the FIFA Congress in May 2024.

FAQ

What are the host cities for the 2027 Women's World Cup?

The eight host cities are Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, Belo Horizonte, Brasília, Fortaleza, Salvador, Recife and Porto Alegre, all in Brazil.

Where is the 2027 Women's World Cup final?

The final is at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro on July 25, 2027. The Maracanã also hosts the opening match on June 24.

Which cities host the 2027 Women's World Cup semifinals?

Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo host one semifinal each. São Paulo also hosts the third-place match.

When is the 2027 Women's World Cup draw?

The final draw is on December 11, 2026, at the Museum of Modern Art in Rio de Janeiro. It sets the groups and tells fans which cities each team plays in.

Do US citizens need a visa for Brazil in 2027?

Brazil currently requires US citizens to get a visa, which can be requested online as an e-visa. Rules and fees can change, so check official Brazilian government sources before booking.

How do I buy tickets for the 2027 Women's World Cup?

Use FIFA's official site, FIFA.com/tickets, where fans can register their interest. General sale dates and prices had not been announced as of late November 2026.
