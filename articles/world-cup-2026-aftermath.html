AFTER THE FINAL:
JULY 19–23, IN ORDER

What happened in the five days after Spain beat Argentina at MetLife Stadium — the post-whistle scuffles and the medal ceremony, FIFA's disciplinary investigation, two very different homecomings, CONMEBOL's push for a 64-team World Cup in 2030, and the fan petitions that cannot do what their signatories want them to do.

Sunday, July 19, 2026
MetLife Stadium, NY/NJ · after the final whistle

Ugly End: Brawl and Turned Backs Mar Spain's Coronation

Spain had just won the World Cup. What followed the final whistle at MetLife Stadium was not a celebration so much as a scramble.

Nahuel Molina struck Rodri in the chest while celebrations were going on around them. Leandro Paredes shoved Eric García by the throat and pushed Gavi to the ground. Argentina head coach Lionel Scaloni pulled Paredes away from the confrontation.

In the middle of it, Lamine Yamal knelt in prayer as the fights broke out around him. That image travelled further than anything else from the night. Yamal and Lionel Messi also shared a warm embrace in the aftermath, despite everything happening around them.

At the medal ceremony, Spain's players formed a guard of honor for the Argentina squad. Argentina did not form one in return, and during Spain's trophy lift the Argentina players turned their backs on the presentation and faced their own supporters instead.

⚠ Disputed — motive not established

Why Argentina's players turned their backs is not settled. Some outlets and fact-checks have reported that the players faced their own supporters out of heartbreak and to acknowledge the travelling fans, rather than as a planned snub of Spain's trophy lift. Others read it as deliberate disrespect.

GO2CUP is not in a position to establish intent, and we are not going to declare one. What is documented is the sequence: Spain formed a guard of honor, Argentina did not, and Argentina's players faced their own end during the lift.

Monday, July 20, 2026

FIFA Opens Investigation Into Final Brawl

FIFA's Disciplinary Committee appointed a prosecutor to investigate the incidents that followed the final whistle at MetLife Stadium. FIFA said its findings would be shared once the investigation is complete.

No sanctions had been announced at the time of writing, and no players had been charged. An appointed prosecutor is the start of a disciplinary process, not its conclusion.

Context

This is the second conduct matter involving the Argentina squad in the closing week of the tournament. FIFA also opened a probe after the semifinal against England, when Lisandro Martínez and Giovani Lo Celso displayed a banner reading “Las Malvinas son Argentinas” in celebration — a breach of FIFA's stadium code of conduct, which prohibits political banners inside stadiums. UK officials objected. FIFA described that probe as standard procedure.

Monday, July 20, 2026
Madrid · Buenos Aires

Two Homecomings — Spain Celebrates, Argentina Consoled

Spain celebrated as world champions in Madrid.

In Buenos Aires, Argentina's squad landed at Ezeiza airport and were welcomed home by supporters who had turned out despite the defeat. A losing finalist does not usually get a reception. This one did.

Two countries, one day, opposite results — and crowds in both.

Tuesday, July 21, 2026

CONMEBOL Pushes a 64-Team World Cup for the 2030 Centenary

CONMEBOL president Alejandro Domínguez said on X that the 2030 World Cup should be expanded to 64 teams — a tournament of 128 matches. The 2030 edition is to be hosted by Spain, Portugal and Morocco, with centenary opening matches staged in Uruguay, Argentina and Paraguay to mark one hundred years since the first World Cup.

FIFA has not confirmed any such expansion. FIFA president Gianni Infantino has said the relevant committees will study proposals after the 2026 tournament.

The other side

The proposal has real opposition. UEFA president Aleksander Čeferin has called a 64-team World Cup “a bad idea.” The 2026 tournament was itself the first at 48 teams, and the debate over whether that expansion improved the competition is far from settled.

Looking ahead to 2030

Spain, Portugal and Morocco, with centenary matches in South America, is a very different fan-travel problem than one North American summer. Wednesday–Thursday, July 22–23, 2026

Fans Launch Replay and Ban Petitions — What FIFA Can Actually Do

A Change.org petition started by an Argentine fan, Gisela Sanchez, alleges that the final's referee, Slavko Vinčić, was biased and had been “bribed.” It passed 80,000 signatures. No verifiable evidence has been provided to support the allegation.

A second petition calls for Argentina to be banned from future World Cups.

What these petitions can and cannot do

Change.org petitions have no regulatory power. They are not a submission to FIFA, they do not trigger any process, and signature counts carry no procedural weight.

FIFA's rules do not provide for replaying a match over disputed refereeing decisions. There is no mechanism for it, regardless of how many signatures a petition collects.

FIFA has given no indication that it intends to act on either petition.

The bribery allegation against the referee is an unproven claim by a petition author. GO2CUP is reporting that the claim exists and that no evidence has been offered for it. We are not repeating it as fact.

Coverage of the petitions has been carried by outlets including Yahoo, beIN Sports and VnExpress. GO2CUP does not link to or embed advocacy or campaign websites.
